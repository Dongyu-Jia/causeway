# Scenario-driven channel review (research round, role `schemaScen`)

*What the role did:* 127 scenarios decomposed into generic channels and checked against the draft table.

Rendered from the role's final report in the research workflow's journal (run `wf_cc7fe8c3-afc`, result `schemaScen`). The text is unchanged except that local absolute paths are written as the spec writes them (`scratchpad/sim_redesign/...`, `scratchpad/...`). It is the report the critic and the designer read; the numbers are the role's own, and the critique ([`critic/critique.md`](../critic/critique.md)) and the spec ([`SIM_DESIGN.md`](../SIM_DESIGN.md)) say which of them survived.

## Summary

I listed 127 scenarios: the 13 built-in specs, 73 historical episodes from 1970 to 2026, 10 official stress scenarios and 31 long-run themes and tail risks. I split each one into generic economic channels, 115 channels in all. I then checked every channel against the lead's draft quarterly event table, the 13-item checklist and the literature panel, which was unfinished when I read its q_meta.json. The draft table covers about a quarter of the 566 scenario–channel pairs where the channel is central to the scenario (32% when start-of-quarter states are counted). Three no-look-ahead diagnostics agree on two things. Most of what the macro factors miss over long horizons is the market multiple. Beyond the 10 core factors, only the excess bond premium (EBP) reliably adds same-quarter explanatory power. So most of the missing channels matter for keeping the factor layer consistent, not as direct drivers of asset prices; I did not test that consistency value. No files in MAIN, RWT or FWT changed (MAIN's git status is clean). The only new downloads were the NY Fed GSCPI file and 43 rate-limited FRED API requests, with the key redacted.

## Key findings

1. Coverage. In the scenario × channel matrix, 566 channels are central to some scenario (core). By draft status: 127 (22%) are in the draft table, 2 are decomposed, 17 are feedbacks the model should learn, 33 belong in the start-of-quarter state s_q, 61 (11%) are partly covered and 326 (58%) are missing. The draft plus s_q covers 32%. 57 of the 566 (10%), spread over 45 scenarios, have no allowed free measured series. Only C04, the Fed's stagflationary adverse scenario, is fully expressible. For the median long-run/tail scenario, none of its core channels is in the draft.
2. What is missing falls into six blocks. (1) Cash flow and valuation: earnings and margins, the corporate tax rate, payout and buybacks, starting valuation, long-run growth expectations. (2) Discount rate and risk appetite: EBP, term premium, real rate, inflation expectations, the expected policy path. (3) Investment and technology: capex, AI/IT investment, productivity, white-collar employment. (4) Housing, credit and balance sheets: house prices, credit supply, household and corporate leverage, commercial real estate, non-bank leverage, funding-market stress. (5) Global: foreign demand including China, global yields, emerging-market stress, foreign official demand. (6) Policy and institutions: tariffs, policy uncertainty, public debt, sector regulation, central-bank independence, sanctions.
3. Top missing channels, with the number of scenarios where each is core: EBP risk appetite 27, physical oil supply 17, term premium 16, funding stress 14 (partly in the draft), news vs realization 14, foreign demand 13, non-bank leverage 11, bank-stress mapping 10, credit supply 9, earnings 9, natural gas and power prices 9, house prices 8, foreign official demand 8, policy uncertainty 8, sector regulation 8. Adding channels greedily on top of the draft plus s_q raises coverage from 32% to 47% after 5, 56% after 10, 70% after 20, 80% after 30 and 90% after all 55 measurable ones. The ranking depends on which scenarios were listed.
4. Episode test (s01). The current transmission model was fitted only on data before each episode and fed the episode's realized factor path. Over 30 out-of-sample episodes from 1985 to 2026 (about 22 non-overlapping), 87% of the US equity residual sum of squares coincides with the residual of the market price/dividend ratio (corr 0.93); the in-sample figure is 89%. This confirms the lead's hypothesis that long-horizon misses are valuation swings. Nasdaq, industrials, healthcare and financials have 73–85% of their misses on that same market multiple. Energy (25%), utilities (21%), REITs (13%) and gold (28%) are mostly sector-specific: energy +0.87 in 2003–08 and +0.64 in 2022, utilities +0.80 in 2003–08, REITs −0.76 in the GFC, gold +0.82 in 2023–26 (log points after removing the market multiple). For financials, healthcare and gold the mean absolute residual is about as large as the mean absolute return, so the macro factors explain almost nothing for them at the episode level.
5. Quarterly screen (s04). I added each of 41 candidate channels alone to the same-quarter 10-factor ridge model, with 40, 40 and 43 test quarters and block-bootstrap 90% intervals. EBP is the only one that gains in all three test decades: +0.044 / +0.215 / +0.073 in mean out-of-sample R² (T1 1995–2004 / T2 2005–14 / T3 2015–26). The gain is still +0.123 in T2 without the GFC, and the interval's lower bound is above zero in T2 and T3. The news-based policy uncertainty index (EPU) adds +0.080 / +0.102 / −0.004. Philadelphia Fed delivery times add −0.011 / +0.038 / +0.040. Same-quarter earnings, house prices, credit, M2, the deficit, transfers and services demand all add about zero or less. Sector pointers, all same-signed in the three decades but with 1,230 asset–candidate–decade cells tested: utilities follow the term premium and natural gas; Nasdaq follows net equity issuance (buybacks) and productivity; industrials follow global activity and delivery times.
6. Timing (s02, quarterly, ridge vs ridge). Adding the next four quarters' realized factor changes gives +0.047 / +0.128 / +0.039, and every interval includes zero. The T2 gain disappears without the GFC (−0.018). Adding the previous quarter's changes hurts: −0.043 / −0.003 / −0.047. The start-of-quarter P/D level adds nothing linearly: −0.151 / +0.012 / +0.007. So news has to come in through measured expectation fields, such as the change in the 2-year yield minus the fed funds rate, the change in Michigan 1-year expectations and the change in the term premium, not through realized future values. The past should enter as levels in s_q.
7. Problems in the draft table itself. (1) bank_stress (0–3) and policy_stance (−2 to +2) need measured mappings. For bank_stress: a composite of Baa−Aaa, the commercial-paper minus T-bill spread, bank deposit growth and discount-window borrowing. For policy_stance: the Taylor-rule gap, plus a separate field for the change in the expected policy path. (2) supply_chain is on the GSCPI scale, and GSCPI starts in 1998-01 (verified by download). The Philadelphia Fed delivery-time index, series DTCDFSA066MSFRBPHI (1968-05, verified), can extend it back. (3) The only shadow-rate measure, Wu–Xia, stops in 2022-02. (4) oil_q mixes supply and demand. (5) ip_q is too narrow for a quarterly model; add real GDP. (6) bank_stress mixes solvency and funding-market stress, which should be separate fields. (7) labor_supply_q is the participation rate and leaves out population and immigration. (8) Quarter-end vs quarter-average changes are not defined. (9) There are only 214 quarters, so every added field costs degrees of freedom.
8. Checklist verdicts. Items 1–7 and 9–11 are confirmed gaps. Item 8 is partly confirmed: index concentration and market liquidity cannot be measured, but buybacks can (Z.1 series NCBCEBQ027S, from 1946). Item 12: the feedbacks are mostly learnable from history, except debt deflation and fiscal dominance, which have no US instance after 1973. Item 13, M2, is not an important gap: it is negative in all three decades and core in no scenario. Gaps the checklist does not list: earnings, the corporate tax rate, EBP, capex and residential investment, tariffs, oil supply as distinct from the oil price, natural gas and food, funding stress, policy uncertainty, real GDP, wages, FX intervention and price controls, labor-force growth, shock persistence, and the quarter definition.
9. Elements with no allowed free measure, with the number of scenarios where each is core: long-run growth expectations (9), market structure and concentration (8), sanctions (7), central-bank gold demand (6), central-bank independence (5), fiscal news (5), natural disasters (5, which can be decomposed into other channels). Also fiscal dominance, carbon pricing, price controls, FX intervention, cyber outages and surprises relative to consensus. They cluster in the AI, dollar-decline, fiscal-dominance and energy-transition scenarios, which must therefore remain scenario-layer assumptions and be labelled as beyond measured history.
10. Measured series verified through FRED metadata or downloaded data. Labor and population: CIVPART, CLF16OV and CNP16OV (1948). Z.1 balance sheets, all from 1945: central-bank assets BOGZ1FL714090005Q, the commercial real estate price index BOGZ1FL075035503Q, broker-dealer assets BOGZ1FL664090663Q, nonfinancial corporate debt BCNSDODNS. Taxes and buybacks: A053RC1Q027SBEA and B075RC1Q027SBEA (1947, corporate tax rate), NCBCEBQ027S (1946-10). Discontinued series: DISCBORR (1959–2020-08), EQTA (1988–2020). Prices, trade and global: CPIMEDSL (1947), PPI pharmaceuticals (1981-06), EXPGSC1 (1947), German 10-year yield (1956-05), Japanese 10-year yield (1989). Potential output and demographics: GDPPOT and NROU (1949), share of population 65+ (1960, annual). Bitcoin CBBTCUSD (2014-12). Already in the factor round's downloads: trade policy uncertainty TPU monthly from 1960; Michigan 5–10-year inflation expectations only continuous from 1990. Two cautions: real nonresidential investment PNFIC1 starts only in 2007 in the current vintage, so the quantity indexes are needed for long history; the prescription-drug CPI series id returned an error.
11. Comparison with the literature panel (q_meta.json). It already holds the term premium, EBP and GZ spread, the CP−bill spread, broker-dealer leverage, the corporate tax rate, tariffs, capex, IT and residential investment, house prices, exports and global activity, EPU and geopolitical risk, Michigan expectations, delivery times, CAPE, P/D, P/E and FCF yield, r* and debt. It lacks physical oil supply, natural gas and food prices, commercial real estate, bank credit losses, household and corporate leverage as states, global yields, emerging-market stress, foreign official demand, white-collar employment, medical prices, power demand, buybacks, and the EPU categories and TPU. It also uses Fernald TFP from the San Francisco Fed, which is not on this round's allowed download list.

## Open questions

1. Untested: whether the proposed fields improve the factor layer's internal consistency, that is, how much they reduce the error in filling in the core factors and slow states when a scenario specifies only some variables. This needs the masked joint model. My tests only covered their direct, same-quarter effect on asset returns, which is small for everything except EBP.
2. The thresholds that map the ordinal fields to measured series need calibrating on history: bank_stress to a composite of Baa−Aaa, the CP−bill spread, deposit growth and discount-window borrowing; policy_stance to the Taylor-rule gap. The lead should decide whether to keep ordinal fields at all.
3. Some useful sources are not on this round's download list: EIA (world crude production, degree days, refining spreads), the Philadelphia Fed (SPF and Livingston expectations, SPF long-run growth), the World Bank and Census. Should new downloads from them be allowed? The factor round's existing files from several of these publishers were read but not refreshed.
4. Several channels that are core to the AI, dollar-decline, fiscal-dominance and energy-transition scenarios have no allowed measure: long-run growth expectations, market structure and concentration, sanctions, central-bank gold demand, central-bank independence, fiscal news, carbon pricing. Confirm they stay as explicit scenario-layer assumptions and bounded overrides, labelled in the UI as beyond measured history.
5. The literature panel uses Fernald TFP from the San Francisco Fed, which is not on the enumerated allowed download list. The lead should confirm before it becomes a model field.
6. The figures for the official stress scenarios are given only to their published order of magnitude and were not checked year by year. All diagnostics use current-vintage data and would differ with first-release vintages.
7. The greedy priority ranking depends on which 127 scenarios were listed. Weighting scenarios by the user's prior probabilities, or using the 13 built-in specs only, could reorder the middle of the list. The top few (EBP, oil supply, term premium, funding stress, expected policy path, foreign demand) are robust across the scenario groups.
