# Housing, fiscal, trade and the consumer: candidate factors (missing-factor round)

Family folder `factors/housing`. Harness v1.1 (panel sha1 3869fdde3437, model_selection 58a9db10ed83, panel to 2026-09). All data downloaded 2026-11-01 from official publishers or the FRED API (`sources.json`); nothing in the project was written. 64 candidates (monthly series built in two alignments, `candidates.csv`), every harness output in `tests.json`, every p value in `pvalues.csv` (51210 rows).

## Bottom line

* **Same-month transmission: nothing earns a place.** Of 768 candidate × asset pairs (reference-month changes, all 12 model assets), 196 (25.5%) pass the task's rule (Δ OOS R² > 0 in ≥ 2 of 3 decades, mean > 0, mean DM t > 0); 18.5% of 720 pure-noise placebo pairs pass it too (`placebo.py`); 1 survives Benjamini–Hochberg at q 0.10 across all pairs (mort_spread_avg × treasury_10y (Δ +0.0019/+0.0023/+0.0007): an averaging-timing artefact on an asset whose model R² is already 0.93–0.95), and none of the 157 pre-specified (mechanism) pairs does (smallest q 0.86). With publication-dated changes (news timing) 155 pairs pass the rule, smallest planned q 0.91. The harness `screen()` calls defense_gdp an *adopt-candidate* (score Δ +0.0126 / +0.0072 / +0.0233); without its two best months per decade it is +0.0052 / -0.0002 / -0.0026 (T3's gain is 2020-04, a month in which the candidate is zero: it only shifts the other coefficients). Every common-factor rule pass (months_supply, rental_vacancy, resinv_share, shelter_cpi, revolving_credit, real_dpi, real_income_ex_transfers, cpi_food_home, ppi_foods, defense_gdp, chargeoffs_all) fails that check.

* **House prices are a missing forecast state, not a missing transmission factor.** Release-dated house-price growth (Freddie Mac HPI y/y, known 2 months later) forecasts the 18-month change in shelter inflation and in services inflation beyond the KEY_STATES baseline plus the target's own current rate: incremental OOS R² +0.33 for shelter (h1 +0.39, h2 +0.22, CW p 0.002) and +0.31 for services (h1 +0.49, h2 +0.13, CW p 0.003; positive in all four origin decades: 85-94 +0.61, 95-04 +0.36, 05-14 +0.09, 15-25 +0.18, ex-GFC +0.21, ex-COVID +0.27). At 12 months the post-2005 half is negative for FMHPI (shelter h2 -0.52): the lead lengthened. Case–Shiller (1987+) gives services 12 m +0.27 with the own-lag baseline (h1 +0.25 on 71 origins, h2 +0.27) and the harness's headline-CPI target +0.17 (h1 +0.07, h2 +0.18). The same state forecasts the 12-month unemployment change: +0.32 (h1 +0.10, h2 +0.35, CW p 9e-04; year-end origins +0.39; baseline alone +0.02), coefficient -0.140 pp per pp of house-price growth (t -7.4). That pass needs the 1972–74 proxy (FMHPI alone: h1 -0.11) and is negative in 1995–2004 (85-94 +0.46, 95-04 -0.46, 05-14 +0.58, 15-25 +0.25, ex-GFC +0.23, ex-COVID +0.40).

* **Other tier-1 forecast states:** lumber PPI y/y → 12-month change of CPI y/y +0.17 (h1 +0.14, h2 +0.19, CW p 0.003, q 0.059; also goods and shelter inflation at 12–18 months, tier 2); food-at-home CPI y/y → CPI +0.08 (all four decades positive); real disposable income y/y → CPI +0.08 (all four decades positive, q 0.089): the household-income feedback shows up as inflation pressure, not as returns; revolving credit y/y → IP +0.05 (h1 only +0.002: marginal). **Tier 2:** bank charge-offs (1985+) → unemployment +0.22 (q 0.177); housing activity → unemployment passes the halves rule (permits +0.12, q 0.111) but is almost all the GFC (85-94 +0.18, 95-04 -0.10, 05-14 +0.38, 15-25 +0.00, ex-GFC +0.06, ex-COVID +0.28).

* **Fiscal and trade: null.** Deficit, debt, net Treasury supply, interest burden, fiscal impulse, receipts, the effective tariff rate, the China import share, import prices and the trade balance neither improve same-month returns nor forecast y10, Treasury returns, CPI or unemployment out of sample (every planned incremental R² ≤ 0 in at least one half; tables below). Post-1973 history has two tariff episodes and no fiscal-dominance episode, so these remain scenario view variables, which the specs need anyway.

* **Calibration of the predictive rule.** Persistent placebo states (AR(1), φ 0.97; 40 series × 19 targets) pass the both-halves rule in 4.1% of pairs and the Clark–West test rejects at 5% in 11.6% (oversized about 2× with overlapping 12-month targets): read CW p values here as roughly twice too small. Planned pairs of this family pass the halves rule in 8.8% (22 of 251).

* **Harness pitfall (worked around, harness not edited).** For a candidate that starts after 1973, `transmission_test`'s `delta`/`dm_t` compare a model fitted on the candidate's months with the base fitted on all months, so they include the effect of dropping the early training months. Over the 20 late starters here the harness shows gold +0.126 R² (DM t 1.4), treasury_10y +0.023 (t 2.5), crude_oil +0.019 (t 2.1); with the base refitted on the same months (`same_sample.py`) the gains are -0.015, -0.0004, +0.0000 (DM t -0.3, -0.4, -0.2). Example: real_retail (1992+) 'gains' +0.247 R² on gold in 2005–14 (DM t 2.1); same-sample −0.000. All verdicts here use the same-sample statistics. Other families using `delta`, `dm_t` or `screen()` on late series should do the same. (Side finding: gold's, Treasury's and crude's base equations fit later decades better without the 1970s–80s months.)

## What this says about the user's question (factor or mechanism?)

* In this family the answer is *mechanism and horizon*, not a missing same-month factor. Housing, credit, fiscal, trade and consumer variables do not explain monthly returns beyond the 10 core factors out of sample. Housing matters through slow, lagged channels the monthly transmission cannot carry: house prices → rents → shelter and services inflation 12–18 months later, and house prices (collateral, construction) → unemployment over the next year. These belong in the one-year forecast's state vector and in the scenario views, where the specs currently have no house-price, shelter or deficit variable at all.

* Household income: real disposable income and real income excluding transfers co-move with the equity residual over 12 months and lead inflation slightly, but do not forecast returns; the feedback the user describes is visible as inflation pressure and as a slow co-movement, not as a tradable same-month factor. The white-collar wage question belongs to the labor family.

## Conventions and checks

* Reference month: the month described; weekly PMMS = last weekly value in the month (published Thursdays, lag 0); quarterly = the quarter's last month, held two months (`hold_quarterly(q, 2)`), never interpolated. Known-at: shifted by the publication lag (ALFRED first-release dates, 90th percentile: starts/permits/new-home sales/retail/CPI/PPI/H.8/MTS 1; G.19, Case–Shiller, FHFA PO, FMHPI, trade, PCE/DPI/saving 2; NIPA customs/imports/GDP components 3; FHFA AT, delinquency, vacancy 4; Z.1, debt/GDP, NIPA net saving 5; DSR 6; foreign holdings 9; `work/release_lags.json`). Today's vintage (house-price indexes and NIPA income are revised; FMHPI has no vintages).

* The October-2025 CPI was never published (shutdown): shelter, food-at-home and import prices are filled in the reference alignment by the neighbours' geometric mean, as the panel does; the known-at alignment holds the last published value.

* Planned assets/targets were set per mechanism in `build_series.py`; after the first run the target lists of the five house-price variants were harmonized to hpi_fm's (y10 and ff added to four), so those q values are not strictly pre-registered. 10 candidates (owners_equity .. cc_delinq) were added after the first screen, mechanism-first.

* `own_predictive.py` reproduces `harness.predictive_test` exactly at h = 12 (max abs deviation 0.0 on r2/t/n); `same_sample.py` reproduces the harness `delta`/`dm_t` exactly for full-history candidates and `delta_same_sample` for all.
