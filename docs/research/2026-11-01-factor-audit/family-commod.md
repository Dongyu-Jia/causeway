* **19 more candidates pass the task's rule but not BH** (copper, base metals, Philadelphia Fed new orders, food and fertilizer prices for staples, PPI electric power for utilities, oil and gas output and drilling for energy...). Their gains are small (mostly below 0.02 in R²) and several have the wrong sign for the stated mechanism (staples rise with farm prices; utilities fall with power prices), so they are listed, not recommended.
* **No forecast state survives BH** (smallest pre-specified Clark-West p 0.003). By the rule only: US oil and gas output y/y for the 12-month oil change (oil: ΔR² +0.144 (h1 +0.021, h2 +0.232), CW p 0.003, year-end +0.066), the EM dollar for gold (ret_gold: ΔR² +0.082 (h1 +0.100, h2 +0.067), CW p 0.003, year-end +0.023), the Kilian index, aluminum and the BLS core-crude index for the IP growth change, the World Bank energy index for goods inflation (goods_yoy: ΔR² +0.046 (h1 +0.061, h2 +0.042), CW p 0.027, year-end +0.039).
* **Unflattering, reported as found.** (1) Commodities do not lead goods inflation beyond what oil and the baseline already carry: of 35 pre-specified goods-inflation tests only `wb_energy` is positive in both halves (also `cli_china`, excluded as look-ahead); copper -0.024, food -0.035, PPI crude -0.039, Philadelphia prices paid -0.034. (2) Global demand explains little of the oil factor month to month: copper + Kilian + G7 CLI R² 0.062 over 1973-2026 (0.013 before 2000, 0.168 since). (3) The Kilian index adds nothing in transmission (energy +0.005/-0.185/-0.000). (4) S&P earnings add nothing month by month (us_equity +0.014/-0.102/+0.013 dated at the quarter end) but their 12-month change tracks the 12-month residual drift (corr with PC1 0.50, t 2.63, dated when known): a slow state, not a transmission factor. (5) The OECD leading indicators' large forecasting gains are look-ahead: with the first-release CLI (ALFRED, 2018-07..2025-09) the US CLI's gain for the 12-month equity return turns from +0.246 to -0.145.
* **Upper bound from equity style factors (not macro, endogenous):** SMB, HML, RMW, CMA and momentum raise the in-sample R² of nasdaq by 0.38, us_equity 0.17, financials 0.16, energy 0.02, utilities 0.02, but out of sample they mostly hurt (energy -0.722/-0.058/-0.070). With the market factor added (the multiple), us_equity gains 0.83 and industrials 0.69: most of what the macro model misses in equities is the market itself, which is the PC1 finding of the miss map, not a commodity or demand factor.

## Engine fit

* Full history from 1973-02 with no gaps (MonthDB condition): every World Bank, PPI, CPI, Philadelphia Fed, G.17, Shiller and EM-dollar series; CPI series needed one interpolated month (2025-10, the shutdown), as the panel does. Late: LNG Japan 1977, EM exports 1981, futures 1985 (ended 2024-03), month-end products 1986, China/Asia CLIs and China exports 1992, Henry Hub month-end and GEPU 1997. The Kilian index lags 2 months (2026-09 not yet published).
* `metals_oil` (1960+) and `usd_em` (1973-02+) can be asset-specific transmission factors in the engine today (energy, industrials). Neither is simulated by the scenario paths; in simulations they would come from the drawn historical months, like the non-simulated extra columns. A scenario view on 'global demand' could later move base metals and the EM dollar together.
* The month-end gas splice and the futures slope would need month-end histories before 1997 / 1985 to be core: none is keyless (NYMEX data are licensed; EIA dropped them in 2024). A long month-end proxy for the EM dollar would need daily H.10 rates of individual EM currencies (FRED DEX* series, most start 1981-1995): possible but not built here.
* Forecast states by the rule only (Kilian level, aluminum, core-crude PPI for IP; energy index for goods inflation; US oil output for oil; Philadelphia activity for unemployment): none passes BH, so none should enter forecast.KEY_STATES without the walk-forward.

## Data notes and caveats

* Monthly averages vs month-end. Pink Sheet, IMF and OITP prices are monthly averages; the assets (except gold) are month-end. An average's monthly change straddles two months of returns, which attenuates same-month effects: copper as a two-month average proxy of month-end (diagnostic, uses the next month) +0.015/+0.049/+0.004 vs the plain average +0.005/+0.019/+0.005 for industrials; Henry Hub month-end vs average corr 0.54; Pink Sheet crude average vs the panel's month-end WTI corr 0.74. The panel's gold is the same LBMA monthly average as the Pink Sheet (corr 0.999997).
* Administered prices: iron ore (annual contract to 2009), US gas (regulated to 1985), European gas, Japanese LNG and Australian coal (contracts) have long runs of unchanged months; their early history is not a market signal.
* Known-at lags used: Pink Sheet, PPI, CPI, CLI, G.17, GEPU 1 month; Kilian, China exports, EM exports 2 months; daily market prices and the Philadelphia survey 0; earnings quarter end + 2 months (hold_quarterly-style step, no interpolation). Data are today's vintage except the ALFRED checks.
* Shiller: the project's copy (econ.yale.edu, 2023-10) ends earnings in 2023-06; the current file (shillerdata.com, saved 2026-10-05) has earnings to 2026-06 and agrees with the old copy except 3 months (max |ΔE| 0.16).
* IMF commodity prices on FRED start in 1992 and are IMF-copyrighted; they were used only to cross-check the Pink Sheet (copper corr 0.9992, aluminum 0.9990). FRED's OECD 'normalized' CLIs end in 2024-01; the amplitude-adjusted ones are maintained. China IP on FRED ended 2023-11 (not used). WPU05 does not exist on FRED.
* Endogeneity flags (harness): silver and the precious index (endogenous market price), copper/gold (contains gold), China CLI and EM exports (check look-ahead: their change leads next month's market, probably from two-sided seasonal adjustment or filtering), CLIs, PPI all commodities, the WB energy index and capacity utilization (spanned by the core factors).
