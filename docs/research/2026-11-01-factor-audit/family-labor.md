# Labor, income and distribution (with white-collar wages): candidate family report

Family folder: `factors/labor`. Harness v1.1 (selftest passed in every run; panel sha1 3869fdde3437, model_selection sha1 58a9db10ed83). Access date of all downloads 2026-11-01. Nothing was written to the project; all scripts ran from `labor/work` with `python -B -I`.

## Bottom line

* **No labor, income or distribution series earns a place as a same-month transmission factor.** Of 60 monthly-change candidates none raises the 10-asset OOS R² in all three decades (T1 1995-2004, T2 2005-2014, T3 2015-2026). 6 pass the lenient rule (2 of 3 decades, positive mean, positive mean pooled HAC t), with mean gains of 0.0008-0.0051 and best combined p 0.124 (BH q 1.00). Pure noise passes the same rule 9% of the time (100 iid normal series), this family 10%. Per asset, 123 of 600 candidate x asset pairs pass (20.5%; noise 19.2%) and none survives Benjamini-Hochberg (min q 1.00).
* **One conditional exception: real PCE on nondurable goods** (the staples' own demand, BEA monthly 1959+). Scored on the project's own iteration-2 test months (131: 2020-03..2020-12 not scored) it raises the 10-asset OOS R² in every decade by more than MIN_GAIN: +0.0083/+0.0406/+0.0054 (vs the base without labor share: +0.0121/+0.0414/+0.0058), but the gain is not significant (combined p 0.13) and with the COVID months scored T3 is -0.1669: the 1973-2014 beta turns the 2020 consumption swings into absurd return predictions.
  Also conditional: real wages and salaries (BEA) is positive in every decade on the 131 months (+0.0017/+0.0070/+0.0026, pooled t 1.44/1.24/0.92, combined p 0.019) but below MIN_GAIN in T1 and T3 and not significant after BH (q 1.00).
* **The labor share should be dropped, not replaced.** Dropping the interpolated core labor share raises the mean OOS R² by +0.0088/+0.0213/+0.0074 (141-month T3; on 131 months T3 is -0.0031). On top of simply dropping it, the 8 replacements tried add at most +0.0092 (labor_share_rt@release in T2), are negative in 18 of 24 candidate-decade cells, and none beats dropping in all three decades. The other labor core factor, unemployment, earns nothing either: dropping it changes the mean OOS R² by +0.0039/-0.0000/+0.0001 on the 131 months, and by +0.0537 in favour of dropping on the 141-month T3 (COVID). Claims, prime-age employment, payrolls or hours do not beat dropping it.
* **Where labor data do carry information is slow and forward-looking: inflation.** The BLS labor share, dated at its release (lag 4), forecasts the 12-month change of CPI inflation in both halves (r2_incr over the KEY_STATES baseline +0.066 in 1985-2004 and +0.149 in 2005-2025, Clark-West p <0.0001, BH q 0.01, year-end origins +0.075/+0.042), with the sign the wage-cost mechanism predicts. It survives lags 5 and 6 and detrending, but **not** a training sample that starts in 1985 (-0.091/-0.148): it is learned from the 1970s wage-price spiral. Household debt service forecasts real consumption growth (negative, as expected), mostly after 2004 (r2_incr +0.018/+0.150). Overall 30 of 1742 predictive tests pass both halves, BH within the family and the year-end check (13 of 1273 on the harness's targets; 14 of the rest are wage measures forecasting wage growth itself); 100 persistent noise predictors reached that standard in 0 of 1900 harness-target tests.
* **White-collar wages are being squeezed in relative terms, but white-collar wage growth predicts nothing** (services inflation r2_incr -0.185/-0.066; no consumption-sensitive asset in both halves). The squeeze in numbers: white-collar P&NS real wage growth +0.29% y/y in 2026-09 (2024-12: +2.02%), -0.80 pp below goods-producing pay growth, white-collar employment -0.26% y/y (negative since 2023), the CPS management/professional earnings premium at 30.8 log points, the lowest since the series starts (2001-01; max 37.6 in 2014-01), and the supervisory premium (all-employee over P&NS hourly earnings) at 14.8, the lowest since its start (2006-03).
* **The capital side is running hot by every distribution measure**: productivity minus real hourly pay at 9.4 (a record since 1947; 2019-12: 1.7), the BLS labor share (lowest since 1947, 2026-04), capital income 26.3% of market personal income (94th percentile since 1973; 2019-12: 23.7%), after-tax profits/GDP 13.1% (highest since 1947, release-dated). None of these forecasts equity returns out of sample: they work before 2005 and fail after (labor share r2_incr for us_equity +0.210/-0.412).

2. **Capital side running hot?** Yes, at records: the productivity-real pay gap (9.4, from 1.7 in 2019-12), the labor share (record low), profits/GDP (13.1%), and households' capital income share (26.3%, peak 26.9% in 2024-01; from 2021-12 to 2026-09 interest income rose from 8.3% to 11.2% of market income and rent from 4.6% to 5.4%, while dividends fell from 11.0% to 9.7%). ### Calibration on noise (09_placebo.py)

* Transmission, 100 iid normal and 100 iid Student-t(3) monthly 'changes' through the same comparison: per-asset rule passes 19.2% / 18.2% of pairs, common rule 9% / 8%, every decade positive 2% / 2%, BH rejections 0 / 0 (max asset z 3.66). Noise lowers the mean score by -0.0048/-0.0036/-0.0023 (the estimation cost of one extra beta).
* Predictive, 100 AR(1) levels (phi 0.97) on the 19 harness targets: both halves 3.7% of tests, with BH q <= 0.10 0.11%, with the year-end check too 0.0%; CW p < 0.05 in 8.1% (monthly overlapping origins over-reject somewhat). Healthcare and gold returns are the most prone to spurious hits from persistent regressors (12% and 10% pass both halves), so the family's healthcare-return hits (prime-age EPOP and LFPR, hours, real PCE, monthly labor share) are listed as exploratory.
* This family: 95 of 1742 predictive tests pass both halves (5.5%), 30 also pass BH and the year-end check (13 on harness targets, 17 on custom targets, of which 14 are wage measures forecasting wage growth itself).

### Training-window artifact for late-starting series (why the verdicts use same-sample comparisons)

The harness's `delta` compares a candidate model fitted on the candidate's months with a base fitted from 1973. For a series that starts in 1980 or 1990 the shorter window alone changes the fit: the household debt service ratio 'raises' gold's OOS R² by +0.162/+0.178/+0.144 (DM t 2.39/1.72/1.72), but with the base refitted on the same months the change is -0.0033/-0.0005/+0.0030. Every 1990+ series (health care, professional services, software wages, temp help) shows the same +0.1 to +0.23 gold artifact. The harness reports `delta_same_sample` but its `dm_t`, `summary` and `screen(dedicated_assets)` use `delta`, so for late starters this family recomputed the DM t on the same sample (own replica of the harness fit; it reproduces the harness's delta, dm_t and delta_same_sample to 5e-07, the 6-decimal rounding of the stored table).

### Short-history series (JOLTS, ECI, U-6, graduates' unemployment, CPS deciles, detailed CES wages, all-employee wages)

They cannot be judged on the standard halves (a 2000+ series has no 1985-2004 origins after 120 training months) and cannot be core transmission factors (MonthDB needs 1973+). On their own split (halves of their own OOS origins) the few 'both halves' results are small or come from the 2021-22 inflation burst (quits -> CPI: cpi_yoy -0.024/+0.295). Roles: scenario view variables (quits, openings, V/U, U-6, graduates' unemployment, ECI by occupation, CPS deciles, supervisory premium) with the long CES proxies named in the table for any transmission use.

## Engine fit and roles

* **Core transmission factor:** none. The only full-history candidates with a gain in every decade need the COVID months excluded from scoring (real PCE nondurables; real wages and salaries and unit labor costs positive in every decade on 131 months but below MIN_GAIN in T1 or T3). If the implementation round wants to try real PCE nondurables, the COVID treatment has to be decided on validation (T1/T2) before looking at T3 again, e.g. the RARE_MONTHS rule applied to fitting and scoring for every spec.
* **Asset-specific factor:** none survives BH; the per-asset passes occur at the noise rate. Tried for each target asset: its own labor cost (retail and trade wages for staples, health-sector wages for healthcare, construction wages for REITs, finance wages for financials, information/software/computer-systems/professional wages for nasdaq), its own demand (real nondurables PCE, real income, health employment, white-collar employment) and its financing (household debt service).
* **Forecast states:** labor share at release -> CPI inflation change (regime-dependent); household debt service -> real consumption growth (new custom target x_real_pce_g); hours of P&NS workers -> wage growth (x_ahe_g, a target forecast.py does not have yet: a link in the wage-price chain rather than an asset forecast). Exploratory and robust: finance-sector wage growth -> 10-year yield / Treasury return / unemployment; real disposable income growth -> core CPI change. The KEY_STATES baseline has no wage or distribution state, so any of these enters forecast.py as a new state, judged in its walk-forward.
* **Scenario view variables:** everything else, notably the white-collar block (wage growth, gaps, premium, employment, graduates' unemployment, occupational premium, supervisory premium), the capital-income share, the productivity-pay gap, claims, JOLTS and U-6. These are the variables the k_shaped / agi_boom / ai_bust specs reason through (concept_gaps.md); as views they are judged only where observed, which is fine for 1964+ CES and 1959+ BEA series.

## Data, alignment and provenance

* Sources (sources.json): 103 FRED series (BLS CES/CPS/JOLTS/ECI/Productivity and Costs, BEA personal income and outlays, DOL ETA claims, Federal Reserve household debt service), 16 ALFRED files (first releases of 15 series to measure publication lags; the 2023-06-01 vintage of TDSP), 5 BLS API v1 queries (of the 15 allowed). FRED's CES values equal BLS's to 0.0 on all 17 overlapping series (2017-01..2026-09), so FRED history was used wherever it exists; BLS supplied detailed P&NS earnings FRED lacks (health care, professional/scientific/technical, management, admin & waste, computer systems design, software publishers; 1990+).
* BEA personal income components without a key: **yes**, as BEA's flat file (apps.bea.gov/national/Release/TXT/NipaDataM.txt, HTTP 200, 36.9 MB, plus SeriesRegister.txt); not downloaded because FRED has the identical series (BEA's API needs a UserID). DOL's ETA 539 file (oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/csv/ar539.csv, 13.4 MB) is reachable without a key but is state-level and not seasonally adjusted from 1971; FRED ICSA/CCSA (DOL national SA, 1967+) were used. www.dol.gov/ui/data.pdf returned 403 and was skipped.
* Reference month (ref, transmission): monthly data at the month described; weekly claims = mean of the weeks ending in the month; quarterly data = the quarter's value from its last month on, held (no interpolation; the change is dated when the quarter is complete). The October 2025 CPS/CPI gap is interpolated (as data/panel.py does) in ref only.
* Known-at (predictive): month by whose end the value was published, measured from ALFRED first-release dates since 2010: CES and CPS lag 1, BEA personal income lag 2 (lag 1 in 83% of months, so 2 is the strict choice), JOLTS lag 2, ECI and CPS quarterly earnings lag 3, Productivity and Costs lag 4 (95%), debt service lag 6; claims = mean of the last 4 weekly values released by month end (initial claims week end + 5 days, continued + 12). Values are today's vintage (as the KEY_STATES baseline).
* Revisions: BEA monthly income is heavily revised; BEA monthly dividends are interpolated by BEA from quarterly data; BEA wages and salaries include bonuses and stock-option exercises (a channel from stock prices to 'income'); Productivity and Costs are revised every quarter and with NIPA updates; TDSP was restarted at 2005Q1 with new sources: the 1980-2004 part is the 2023-06-01 vintage scaled by the 2005-2022 mean ratio (1.184; overlap correlation 0.985, max deviation after scaling 0.91 pp).
* White-collar wage index: Tornqvist chain of P&NS hourly earnings in information, financial activities and professional & business services, weighted by payroll shares (P&NS employees x earnings); the $ level for the premium is the employment-weighted average (composition-dependent). P&NS workers are about 80% of private employees; the all-employee (2006+) version includes supervisors.

* `delta`/`dm_t`/`summary`/`screen(dedicated_assets)` credit a shorter training window to a late-starting candidate (see above); this family's verdicts use same-sample comparisons from an own replica (validated).
