# Verification of the missing-factor round (adversarial)

Access date 2026-11-01. Harness v1.1, panel sha1 3869fdde3437 (last month 2026-09). Every number below is generated by `scripts/s13_report.py` from `verified.json` / `work/*.json`. Nothing in the project was written (see 'Project state' for one transient git lock).

## Bottom line

* **Multiple testing over all six families.** Transmission: 3960 candidate x asset tests (same-sample Diebold-Mariano, Stouffer over decades), Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.10 keeps **17**; the 10-asset score (396 tests) keeps **2**. Prediction: 5634 candidate x target tests; the families' Clark-West statistic keeps 17, the MSE-gain (DM) statistic keeps 0, and Clark-West calibrated on 300 persistent placebos keeps 1.
* **Confirmed transmission improvements (4):** the ACM term-premium change x 1{core CPI y/y > 4% last month} (`tp10_x_core4`), the Gilchrist-Zakrajsek credit spread (`gz`), the timing-fixed core spread (`spread_fixed`, a data fix that replaces f_spread) and base metals relative to oil for energy (`metals_oil`). Each survives pooled BH, all three decades, all rolling windows, the 2008-09/2020 exclusion and circular-shift placebos (the one-month lag/lead versions are much weaker), and each was rebuilt exactly from the raw download. Their caveats are below; the most important: the TP term's 1995-2014 gains come from cleaner normal-regime betas (no test month is in the regime), and GZ is partly market-driven.
* **No forecast state is confirmed.** The strongest (real-estate-loan growth -> CPI change, the release-dated labor share -> CPI change) survive every robustness check including ALFRED real-time data, but not BH once the over-sized Clark-West test is calibrated, they are learned from the 1970s (training from 1985 kills the first half), and added to KEY_STATES they do not improve the engine's one-year forecast at all (CRPS and rank IC unchanged at the build's bandwidth; the validation rule then picks a narrower bandwidth that is worse on the test origins).
* **Refuted claims include** real disposable income -> CPI (detrended it loses, fails 1985 training), the house-price -> unemployment link (1995-2004 decade -0.46), bank credit -> CPI (2005-14 -0.12), the TP x headline-CPI>3% variant (T2 gain is 2008-09), real PCE nondurables (COVID), defense/GDP (2020-04), and every late-starting 'gold factor' (training-window artifact: harness-style DM would add 45 spurious BH rejections, 28 of them gold).

## 8. Issues found

* **Over-sized Clark-West test.** Every family used CW for prediction; on persistent placebos it rejects at 5% up to 16% of the time. With the plain MSE-gain test no pair survives pooled BH (min DM p 0.0014); with calibrated CW one does. Forecast-state claims based on raw CW q values (labor, rates, housing, commod) overstate significance.
* **Training-window artifact (confirmed).** Late starters 'help' gold/energy against a base fitted from 1973; same-sample comparisons remove it (section 2a). Families worked around it correctly; `screen()` and raw `dm_t` do not.
* **Duplicated targets.** `oil` and `ret_crude_oil` are the same 12-month series (max diff 8e-16); families counted both as separate hits (rates: re_loans -> oil and -> ret_crude_oil).
* **Slow level predictors.** Level/share predictors (exports/GDP, the computers investment share, real disposable income growth) pass the halves rule and CW but lose once the trailing 10-year mean is removed: their evidence rests on a few decade-scale swings (a pure time trend does not reproduce them). A detrending check should be standard for levels.
