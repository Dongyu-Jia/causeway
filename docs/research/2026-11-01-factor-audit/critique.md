# Critique: missing factors, missing mechanisms, or the model? (completeness review of the missing-factor round)

Access date 2026-11-01. Harness v1.1 (panel sha1 3869fdde3437, model_selection 58a9db10ed83, panel to 2026-09).
Every table below is generated by `scripts/c09_tables.py` from the outputs in `work/`; nothing is typed by hand.
Nothing in the project directory was written: the project's code was only imported read-only with `python -B -I`
(the replica in `scripts/clib.py` matches `harness.base_r2`, `drop_test`, `transmission_test` and
`oos_residuals` to at most 1.6e-15; the project's own `walkforward.compare` reproduces all nine model-change rows
of `diagnostics.json` exactly, at the file's 6-decimal precision). The verifier runs in parallel, so the
families' verdicts are taken as the families stated them; where I recomputed a number myself it is marked "(c0x)". New downloads: one French Data Library file
and one FRED series (`sources.json`); the FRED key is in no file.

Windows (harness): T1 = test 1995-2004 trained 1973-1994; T2 = test 2005-2014 trained 1973-2004; T3 = test
2015-01..2026-09 trained 1973-2014 (141 months; "131 m" drops 2020-03..12 as the project's own iteration-2 test).
Score = mean out-of-sample R^2 over the 10 score assets, against the training-window mean price return.

## Bottom line

1. **The user is right about where the gains are.** Factor fixes beat model changes by a wide margin. The
   families' useful set (the core spread rebuilt with consistent month-end timing, the GZ credit spread, the
   ACM term-premium change active only when CPI inflation is above 4%, and the interpolated labor share dropped;
   "U4") raises the score by +0.090 / +0.243 / +0.081 (c05). The best model change on the base, ridge 0.05,
   gives +0.0104 / -0.0025 / -0.0060. The small MLP gives -0.020 / +0.110 / +0.005, and shorter windows lose
   0.17-0.39 in T3. U4's gain carries over to six industries the project never used for selection (they are
   not among its assets): +0.184 / +0.246 / +0.100, with 6 of 6 better in every decade (c07c).
2. **But the ceiling is low, so the hypothesis is only partly right.** I put all 294 admissible candidates
   (the six families' and the project's own) into one ridge, lasso or principal-components model, chose the
   penalty on T1/T2 only, and scored T3 once. The T3 score is 0.18-0.23 depending on the method (lasso with one
   common penalty: 0.228), against 0.216 for the fixed core alone and 0.172 for the base.
   Even an oracle penalty chosen on T3 itself reaches only 0.266. On average **about 71% of the monthly
   price-return variance in 2015-2026 stays unexplained**: about 54-66% for the market,
   industrials, nasdaq, energy and REITs, 74-85% for staples, utilities, financials and healthcare, and all of
   it for gold.
3. **What is left is mostly the market's own risk-premium moves and a few crisis months, not a missing macro
   series.** PC1 of the remaining residuals still carries 0.52 of their variance (base:
   0.56), and it correlates 0.74 with the market P/D change. The same-month P/D change
   (endogenous) explains 73% of us_equity's residual after the honest bound. Ten months carry 33-56% of each
   asset's T3 residual sum of squares. Even with hindsight, coefficients re-estimated decade by decade would
   remove only 6-29% of the residual (adjusted R^2, c04b). The engine already carries this remainder through
   its resampled residuals, and it should say so in the UI.
4. **The user's own channels act slowly, through inflation, not as same-month factors.** These are
   white-collar pay, the hot capital side and household income. The families find them as forecast states:
   the release-dated labor share and real-estate-loan growth forecast the change in CPI, house prices forecast
   shelter and services inflation and unemployment, and household debt service forecasts consumption. They do
   not show up as monthly transmission factors. The household-consumption leg does show up, weakly, in the
   asset that prices it, retail stocks, which the project does not model. Real PCE added to Shops gives
   +0.0084 / +0.0265 / +0.0103 on top of U4 (z 2.02, p 0.022, BH q 0.92 over 42 tests; c07).
5. **Once the factors are fixed, model changes start to help a little.** On top of U4, ridge 0.05 adds
   +0.0086 / +0.0216 / +0.0045, above the 0.003 bar in every decade. The MLP16 gain is positive in T2 and T3 for
   all four seed sets but changes sign in T1. Windows, lags and the full regime spec still lose (c05, c05b).
6. **Gaps no family closed.** I checked these here: the release timing of the core macro factors, a regime
   interaction on every single core factor, a credit-stress interaction, and the month-end dollar. All are null
   or worse. Still untested:
   * first-release (real-time) core macro data;
   * the dedicated drivers for healthcare (medical CPI, pharma PPI) and utilities (degree days);
   * activity nowcasts (CFNAI, ADS);
   * net payout and broker-dealer leverage (Z.1);
   * global yields;
   * sticky/median CPI as CPI forecast states;
   * a month-end gold price, for which no allowed source exists;
   * consumer discretionary, corporate credit and international equities as assets.

   The ranked list is in section 5.

### 1e. Process gaps

* **No untouched test period is left.** The families screened on T1-T3, and the project's last adopt
  decision already used T3. My out-of-universe check (the six industries the project does not use) is the
  only remaining honest replication of U4. It is cross-sectional, not a new time period. Alternative: record
  live predictions from 2026-10 on and judge them next year.
* **The harness credited a shorter training window to late-starting candidates.** Every family hit this
  (`delta`, `dm_t`, `screen` use the full-window base) and worked around it with a same-sample comparison. The
  harness should default to the same-sample comparison.
* **BH ran within each family only.** Cross-family BH is the verifier's job. Candidate counts are large:
  rates 21,410 p-values, labor 39,922, housing 51,210 rows.

How to read the bound:
* **Searching 294 series costs about as much as it finds.** With the penalty chosen honestly, the kitchen
  sink changes the fixed core's T3 score by -0.034 to +0.013 across the 12 fits (lasso, common penalty:
  +0.012). It is worse than the hand-picked U5 in every decade (T1 0.031 vs 0.066, T2 0.061 vs 0.140, T3 0.228 vs 0.256). Shrinkage spreads the weight that U5
  puts on GZ over 199 series.
* **The lasso finds the same few series the families found.** Among its largest coefficients, without being
  told: GZ (6 of 10 assets), the term-premium x core-inflation interaction (3), month-end Henry Hub gas
  (energy, utilities) and the EM dollar (3). It also picks EPU (5) and real PCE nondurables (6), which the
  families rejected one at a time. Healthcare gets no candidate at all.
* **The in-sample ceiling overstates.** OLS on all 208 regressors over 1973-2026 gives R^2 0.46-0.61
  (adjusted 0.20-0.42; gold 0.37 adjusted, yet at most -0.014 out of sample).

**What is left (c04, c04b): the useful set's expanding-window OOS residuals, 1985-2026.**
* "SSE kept" = residual sum of squares of U5 divided by the base's (1985-2026, and 2005-26).
* P/D change, PC1 and equity factors are same-month R^2 of the residual (all endogenous: the market itself).
* hindsight beta = R^2 of the residual on the model's own regressors with one beta over 1985-2026, or with a
  separate beta per decade (adjusted). It is the most any coefficient-estimation change could recover with
  the same regressors.
