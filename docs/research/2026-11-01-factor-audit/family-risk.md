## Bottom line

1. **The biggest problem in this family is a mis-measured core factor, not a missing one.** Before 1986 the panel's `s_spread` = Moody's Baa **monthly average** minus the **month-end** 10-year yield (DBAA starts only in 1986). Its monthly change then correlates 0.11 with the same month's us_equity return and -0.28 with the previous month's (wrong sign, wrong timing). Rebuilt from FRED's weekly Moody's and weekly 10-year yields (last week of each month, same week for both legs; unchanged from 1986), `spread_fixed` replaces `f_spread` with score Δ OOS R² +0.016 / +0.089 / +0.038 in T1 / T2 / T3, pooled HAC t 1.26 / 1.76 / 2.19, 7/9/10 of 10 assets better. The us_equity spread beta in 1973-1999 moves from -0.031 (t -2.8) to -0.062 (t -4.2), closer to the -0.088 of 2000-2026: part of the 'beta instability' the miss map reports is this timing error.
2. **The GZ credit spread (Federal Reserve, 1973+) is the one dedicated credit factor that earns its place**: added to the base it raises the mean OOS R² by +0.073 / +0.191 / +0.053 (pooled t 1.78 / 2.04 / 2.84; 9/9/9 of 10 assets better), and it still adds +0.052 / +0.098 / +0.026 on top of the timing-fixed spread (pooled t 1.68 / 1.75 / 1.48). It is a corporate-bond market price (same-month asset returns explain 0.26 of its monthly change, 0.30 with the previous month's), i.e. the same kind of variable as the core spread, not an equity price.
3. **Its famous half, the excess bond premium, does not** add to same-month transmission (-0.011 / +0.041 / -0.001; replacing the spread with it loses -0.018 / -0.028 / -0.098). It forecasts the next 12 months' unemployment and IP change in both halves, but weakly (Clark-West p 0.119 and 0.118).
4. **Everything else either uses prices to explain prices, leaks the future, or adds nothing out of sample.** VIX, VXO and realized volatility 'explain' a lot (VXO/VIX score Δ +0.355 in T2) because they are the equity market's own volatility. The NFCI family's history is a smoothed, revised estimate: with ALFRED first releases (2011-2026) today's month-end change loads 0.25 (t 6.1) on **next** month's first-release change. Policy uncertainty, its 11 categories, trade-policy uncertainty and geopolitical risk give no reliable same-month gain for any asset once the training sample is held fixed, and geopolitical risk does not explain the oil factor (same-month t 0.19 for GPR acts, 0.39 for Saudi GPR).
5. Multiple testing: of the 253 headline tests of non-endogenous candidates (one per hypothesis), Benjamini-Hochberg at q = 0.10 keeps 8: all of them `spread_fixed` and `gz`. All 15747 p values are in `pvalues.csv` for the cross-family BH.

## 1. The core credit spread is mis-timed before 1986 (a measurement fix, the user's point exactly)

* Construction: `panel.py` builds `lvl_baa` from DBAA (daily, month-end) only from 1986-01 and from the Moody's monthly average before; `lvl_y10` is DGS10 month-end from 1962. So for 1973-1985 the spread change mixes a mid-month corporate yield with a month-end Treasury yield.
* Symptom: 1973-1985 corr of Δs_spread with us_equity, same month 0.108, previous month -0.282. After the fix (last weekly average of the month for both legs): 0.041 and -0.190 (corporate yields still lag; the weekly average ends up to six days before month end). The fixed and panel spread changes correlate 0.83 over 1973-1985; identical from 1986 (max difference 0.0).

## 2. GZ spread, EBP and the default component

* GZ in-sample (1973-2026): us_equity R² 0.166 → 0.264; coefficients negative for all nine equity series (HAC t -2.9 to -6.3), gold +0.017 (t 1.1): the expected signs. A one-sd monthly widening lowers us_equity by 1.90%.
* Where it comes from: the GZ gain is largest in T2 (the GFC). With both models trained only on 1986+ data, GZ adds +0.020 / +0.051 / -0.037 (same-sample), so part of the full-sample gain comes from the badly timed pre-1986 core spread it replaces; on top of the timing-fixed spread it still adds in all three decades (table above).
* EBP vs default component: the EBP is GZ minus a fitted default spread (distance to default from equity value and volatility, regression re-estimated on the whole sample every month, so the split itself is revised and uses future data). Neither half alone matches the total: EBP -0.011 / +0.041 / -0.001, default part -0.013 / +0.019 / +0.012. The decomposition adds nothing beyond the total for transmission; for forecasting, see below.
* Residuals: ΔGZ correlates -0.435 (t -6.2) with PC1 of the OOS residuals (the 'market multiple' component, 56% of residual variance), significant in both halves for us_equity, nasdaq, energy, industrials, financials.
* Revisions: only today's vintage exists (the Fed says the whole history may revise each month; a data-provider change from September 2026). The GZ total is an average of observed bond spreads, so revisions come from sample composition, not from a model.

## 3. NFCI family: look-ahead in today's vintage

* `endogeneity_check`: month-end ΔNFCI correlates -0.183 with the NEXT month's us_equity return (t -4.7); leverage subindex -0.247. A monthly change that leads next month's market is the signature of smoothing / re-dating.
* ALFRED first releases (NFCI, ANFCI, 2011-08..2026-08, 181 months): first-release vs today's month-end changes correlate 0.78 (NFCI) and 0.50 (ANFCI); mean absolute level revision 0.15 and 0.24 sd. Regressing today's monthly change on the first-release changes of the same and the NEXT month: NFCI coefficients 0.61 (t 8.1) and 0.25 (t 6.1), R² 0.72. Today's history carries next month's news.
* Out of sample the family looks good in T2 and bad in T3 (NFCI +0.018 / +0.112 / -0.072), and the predictive gains (unemployment, IP) use the revised history. No real-time NFCI exists before May 2011, so no honest long version can be built: report only.
* STLFSI4 (1994+) and KCFSI (1990+) contain VIX and spreads, are partly market-driven and lose out of sample (same sample).

## 4. Volatility: prices explaining prices

* VIX (1990+), VXO-spliced (1986+) and realized volatility (1926+) give the largest same-month 'gains' in the family (VXO/VIX score Δ T2 +0.355 / T3 +0.077; realized vol +0.043 / +0.076 / +0.021). They are the equity market's own second moment: VIX is an option price on the S&P 500 (same-month asset returns explain 0.49 of ΔVIX, 0.55 with the previous month's), realized volatility is the sum of the month's squared daily market returns (the harness's linear R² of 0.12 misses this because the link is |r|, not r). In a scenario they are outcomes, so they cannot be drivers.
* As predictors (known at month end, no look-ahead) VIX forecasts 12-month us_equity log returns on top of the KEY_STATES baseline in both halves (incremental R² +0.203 / +0.162), but h1 has only 49 monthly origins (VIX starts 1990) and the baseline itself is poor for returns; the variance risk premium does not (-0.004 / -0.053).

## 5. Policy uncertainty and trade policy

* News EPU (main index, 1900+: the 6-paper historical index to 1984, identical by construction, then the 10-paper index) has the right signs in sample (us_equity t -3.6, gold t 2.5) but a weak OOS gain: +0.037 / +0.008 / -0.003, pooled t mean 0.50 (p 0.193).
* Slow-factor pointer: 12-month ΔEPU correlates -0.311 (t -3.9) with 12-month sums of PC1 of the residuals and is significant in both halves for us_equity, nasdaq, energy, industrials. EPU rises in downturns, so this is a pointer for a slow state, not proof.
* Dedicated categorical hypotheses all fail on the same sample: tpu→industrials +0.001/+0.006/-0.002; epu_health→healthcare -0.020/+0.022/-0.007; epu_entitle→healthcare -0.036/+0.023/-0.005; epu_regulation→energy +0.018/+0.014/-0.057; epu_regulation→utilities +0.004/+0.027/-0.087; epu_regulation→financials +0.038/+0.043/-0.117; epu_finreg→financials +0.004/-0.016/-0.030; epu_trade→industrials -0.001/+0.005/-0.047; epu_sovdebt→gold +0.005/-0.011/-0.007.

## 6. Geopolitical risk, oil and gold

* Oil factor (f_oil, same month): ΔGPR 0.007 (t 0.25), threats t 0.73, acts t 0.19, historical t -0.33, Saudi t 0.39, Russia t 1.34; correlations with the project's identified oil-supply shock e_oil_supply ≤ 0.054; expanding-window OOS R² for f_oil all negative (-0.008 to -0.001). Geopolitical risk does not explain monthly oil moves.
* The exceptions are the wars: of the 15 largest |f_oil| months since 1985, GPR acts jumped in 1990-08 (Iraq-Kuwait; Saudi GPR Δlog 3.49) and 2026-03 (acts 1.69, Saudi 1.82, oil +0.429); most others are demand, OPEC or COVID months with GPR flat or falling. A rare-event view, not a monthly factor.
* Gold: same-month geopolitical changes add nothing once the sample is held fixed (GPR threats → gold +0.015 / +0.014 / +0.001); the historical acts index forecasts 12-month gold returns in both halves (incremental R² +0.077 / +0.044, CW p 0.005, BH q 0.106): a pointer.
* Not pre-specified, no mechanism claimed: the mortgage spread passes the rule for gold in every decade (+0.048 / +0.058 / +0.054, combined z 3.54). A timing check (`scripts/gold_timing_check.py`) shows it is the mortgage rate itself (+0.024 / +0.099 / +0.050), not the 10-year leg measured at a different time of month (last-week 10y +0.000 / -0.046 / +0.000; monthly-average 10y -0.036 / -0.063 / -0.002). Left to the gold and housing families.
* **Gold training-window effect (side finding).** Every candidate that starts in 1985 'improves' gold by about +0.15 OOS R². That is not the candidate: the base model refitted only on 1985-02 onward improves gold by +0.139 / +0.196 / +0.148 (DM t 1.30 / 1.81 / 1.60) in T1 / T2 / T3, while it hurts most other assets in T3. Gold's 1973-1984 relation (the 1979-80 spike) distorts its betas. Worth a gold-specific window test in the walk-forward.

## 7. Surveys: bank lending standards and the anxious index

* SLOOS (quarterly from 1990, held from release to release, lag 1): no target improves in both halves (unemployment, IP, the spread, us_equity, financials, REITs). The best case, C&I large-firm standards → unemployment, gains +0.149 in h1 (only 45 monthly origins, 2001-04..2004-12) and loses -0.039 in h2. Too short for a 12-month forecast state.
* SPF anxious index (1968+, re-dated from 'quarter for decline' to the survey quarter, then lag 1): forecasts the 12-month unemployment change in both halves (+0.056 / +0.040, CW p 0.067) with the expected sign; weak.
* ICE BofA high-yield and BBB OAS: FRED now serves only 2023-10-31 onward (ICE licence, 'only 3 years of observations'): untestable.

## 8. Which candidates use prices to explain prices (plain statement)

* **Equity prices explaining equity prices (not admissible as transmission factors):** VIX, VXO/VIX, realized volatility, the variance risk premium; the GZ default component (distance to default is built from equity value and equity volatility); STLFSI4 and KCFSI (contain VIX and bank-stock volatility); the NFCI risk subindex (equity volatility among its inputs).
* **Look-ahead:** the whole NFCI family (today's vintage is smoothed and revised; ALFRED evidence above).
* **Other markets' prices (the same footing as the existing core factors y10, spread, usd):** GZ spread, EBP (bond prices minus an equity-based fit), Baa-Aaa, spread_fixed, the paper-bill and TED spreads, the mortgage spread. The harness flags none of them 'endogenous' (largest same-month R² on asset returns: GZ 0.30).
* **Not prices:** EPU and its categories, TPU, GPR (newspaper text), SLOOS, the anxious index. EMV is text but tracks realized volatility.

## 9. Engine fit

* Full history from 1973-02 (MonthDB condition): spread_fixed (1962+), GZ, EBP, default component (1973-02+), Baa-Aaa, paper-bill (1971+; 9 months filled from AA financial CP), mortgage spread (1971+), NFCI family (1971+), realized vol (1926+), news EPU (1900+), TPU (1960+), historical GPR and its parts (1900+).
* Late: VIX 1990, VXO 1986 (to 2021), STLFSI4 1994, KCFSI 1990, EPU three-component / categorical / EMV / recent GPR 1985, TED 1986-2021, SLOOS 1990. Honest long proxies exist for recent GPR (the historical GPR, level corr 0.96 since 1985) and VIX (realized volatility, corr 0.83), but neither earns a place, and VIX/RV are endogenous anyway. Categorical EPU has no long proxy.
* Recommended roles: replace f_spread with spread_fixed (a rebuilt core factor); test GZ as an added or replacing core factor in the walk-forward (selection on validation, gate on the untouched test); EBP, the anxious index, historical GPR acts (gold) and entitlement EPU (healthcare) only as forecast-state candidates; everything else as scenario view variables at most.

## 10. Harness issues and how they were handled (the harness was not edited)

* `screen()` labels 17 late-starting candidates 'dedicated-asset: [gold]'. Its dedicated test uses `delta`/`dm_t` against the 1973 base, so every 1985 series inherits the gold training-window effect (section 6); the only full-history series with that label, mortgage_spread, is the unexplained gold result of section 6. Work-around: `run_tests.py` re-implements the comparison with the base refitted on the candidate's months (`delta_same_sample` plus its own DM / CW / pooled t; replication error vs the harness 0.0 and 0.0), and every verdict requires the gain on both comparisons.
* `screen()`'s oos_T1 includes too-short decades (e.g. KCFSI T1 with 59 training months); this report counts a decade only with ≥ 108 training months (the 1985-02 series have 119 in T1).
* `endogeneity_check` is linear, so realized volatility (a function of |r|) passes as 'ok'; it is classed as endogenous here by construction.
* A common-factor HAC t is not in the harness: the pooled t (Newey-West t of the per-month contribution to the score delta) is computed with the harness's own fitting functions and checked against its per-asset deltas.
