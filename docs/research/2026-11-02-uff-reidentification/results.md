## Why

- **The puzzle is in the reduced-form lags, not the impact column.**
  - Same-quarter correlation of the rff innovation with real oil: 0.015. The +50bp impact on oil is +0.33 (r0) and
    0 (b1); on Michigan −0.007 (r0) and 0 (b2).
  - Core's path moves ≤ 0.002 under b1 and b2.
  - Posterior sum of rff's lag coefficients: headline CPI's equation +0.256 (sd 0.074), services +0.077 (0.051).
- **(a) restricts the posterior hard.**
  - It keeps 1.37% of the stable proposals (100 of 7,275; all proposals: 0.31%; no fallbacks). The forward fit's
    rejection rate goes from 0.778 to 0.997.
  - It moves the lag sums: services' to −0.015, headline's to +0.159. Steady states barely move (rff 1.01 → 0.93).
  - The point model is unchanged, so F9 and T-C are unchanged.
- **The C′ cycle module erases the IP channel in step mode.** In quarters that state no growth, IP growth is the
  block draw. A policy shock's analytic IP response after impact is exactly 0 in the cycle system, against −0.18 pp
  cumulative over q1–8 in the VAR. Unemployment then responds only through its own slow lags.
- **Exploratory (post hoc, not a candidate).** Draws whose u peak falls in q4–12:
  - r0: 1% in the cycle system, 36% in the VAR;
  - a: 21% in the cycle system, 84% in the VAR.
- **Costs of b2.** It moves Michigan's own response to a Fed surprise from impact to the lags. It shifts the fiscal
  shocks' (u_def, u_tr, u_ptax) impact columns by 7–24% relative, through the satellite covariance. b1 leaves them
  exactly unchanged. Estimation is untouched (Σ_u, Φ, draws identical).
